# The architecture

**Status: under construction.** The architecture is described; its sizes, formats and several
policies are open (listed at the end), and nothing is built.

## The DUT and its devices

In Waveflow's terms ([Devices and the DUT](../../guide/systems/devices.md)), a **device** is one or more
kernels and the adaptors that join them to what is not a stream, and becomes one sub-top in the
system's Verilog; its kernels together are one HLS top. The **DUT** is the devices, the memories and the
interconnect: the streams between devices and the crossbar.

| | Kernels | Adaptors |
|---|---|---|
| **Front end** | the capture and power detector | a slave adaptor with views: the ring's BRAM window (port A on the bus; the kernel keeps port B), the threshold registers, the counters |
| **Controller** | the controller, the two pools' `StreamDispatch` and `StreamArbiter` | a slave adaptor for the host: its threshold registers, the result queue, the summary interrupt |
| **Systolic unit**, $N_s$ copies | the unit | a memory reader (`MemRStream`) on the crossbar |
| **Vector unit**, $N_v$ copies | the unit | a memory reader on the crossbar |
| *Interconnect* | the AXI crossbar (the units' reads to the ring; the host's access to both slave adaptors), and the direct streams: notifications and state messages between the front end and the controller, jobs and results between the controller and each unit | |

**Each unit is its own device.** A device's kernels synthesize together as one HLS top, so if the
controller's device held the units, changing $N_s$ would synthesize every unit again. As separate
devices, a unit is synthesized and calibrated once, and a pool of any size instantiates copies of the
same sub-top: every pool size in the exploration reuses that synthesis and its calibration records.

**The testbench**, outside the DUT: the **sample source**, which drives the front end's sample stream
(the workload's samples, replayed from a file at RTL), and the **host**, the A53 as a `SwHost` thread
(with its generated C++ twin at RTL), which sets the thresholds and reads the results and summaries
through the crossbar.

**One gap remains:** the capture kernel writes the ring through port B, and a generated kernel cannot
yet bind a BRAM window's port B as a declared endpoint (`MemSlaveBramWindow` is not yet joined to
`BramIF`, `waveflow/hw/mm_bram.py`). That is the next extension after the full-rate read port
(`plans/bram_fullrate_port.md`, *Out of scope, next*).

Two rules shape the design:

- **The sample path never stalls.** Samples arrive from the converters at a fixed rate, and nothing
  that faces a converter may wait. The capture and power detector therefore never reads memory, never
  waits for a reader, and never blocks on its outputs.
- **Reading happens downstream.** Every read of the samples goes through the ring's other port, over
  the crossbar, where the downstream jobs compete with each other. That is where the read delay and the
  contention are, and they are part of what is measured.

## The capture and power detector

One streaming kernel (a `FreeRunMod`) at the head of the chain. It writes every sample into the ring
and computes each channel's energy **in the same pass, as the samples go by**. It makes no detection
decision: it only decides, from energy and state, whether downstream should look at a block.

### State

- **A state per channel**, one of three:
  - `absent`: no emitter is being tracked. The channel is notified when its energy is **above** the
    arrival threshold.
  - `active`: an emitter is being tracked. The channel is notified when its energy is **below** the
    departure threshold.
  - `pending`: a notification is out and its answer has not come back. The channel is not notified
    again until downstream sets it to `active` or `absent`.

  All channels start `absent`. Only a state message from downstream changes `active` or `absent`; the
  kernel itself moves a channel to `pending` when it notifies.
- **The block index**, counting blocks since start.
- **The ring's write slot**, the block index modulo $R$.

`pending` is what keeps one emitter from causing two searches. Without it, an absent channel whose next
block is also above the arrival threshold would be notified again before downstream had answered the
first notification. It also sets the late-block policy of [the problem](problem.md#what-can-be-designed):
while a channel is pending, its newer blocks are skipped.

### Interfaces

| Interface | Direction | What |
|---|---|---|
| Sample stream | in | blocks of $B \cdot C \cdot n_{rx}$ samples, at line rate |
| Ring, port B | out, write only | every sample, into the current slot |
| Registers (memory-mapped, host) | in | the arrival and departure thresholds, common to all channels for now, changed rarely |
| Counters (memory-mapped, host) | out, read only | blocks seen; notifications sent and dropped, per channel |
| State messages | in, a queue | from downstream: channel $j$ → `active` or `absent` |
| Notifications | out, a credit stream | to downstream: one message per notified channel-block |

**The thresholds are stored already scaled** by the host: the register holds the threshold on the
**sum** $\sum |x|^2$ over a channel's block ($B \cdot n_{rx}$ terms), not on the mean, so the kernel
compares without dividing.

### Operation, per block

1. **For each sample word:** write it to the ring through port B, into the current slot, at its
   channel's place in the slot's layout ([below](#the-ring)); add $|x|^2$ of each element into the
   channel's accumulator, with a lane loop over the word's antennas, unpacked with the generated array
   utilities. The slot's **header word** (the block index) is written before the block's first sample.
2. **At the block's end**, with the $C$ energies complete, read the two thresholds.
3. **For each channel**, by its state:
   - `absent` and energy ≥ arrival threshold → an **arrival** notification;
   - `active` and energy < departure threshold → a **departure** notification;
   - `pending`, or neither condition → nothing.
4. **For each notification, in order:** if a credit is free, send the message and set the channel to
   `pending`. If not, **drop it**: count the drop, and leave the channel's state as it was.
5. Reset the accumulators; move to the next slot.

**A dropped notification repairs itself.** The channel's state is unchanged, so if its energy is still
past the threshold, the next block notifies again. What the drop costs is delay, which is measured.

**The notification order matters when credits are short.** If several channels qualify in one block and
fewer credits are free, a fixed channel order always favours the low channel numbers. Options:
departures first (cheap and important), round-robin over channels, or highest energy first. It is a
design parameter.

### The notification

| Field | What |
|---|---|
| `tx_id` | the notification's id, echoed by downstream's state message |
| `block` | the block index (the delay is measured from it) |
| `channel` | $j$ |
| `type` | arrival or departure |
| `energy` | the measured sum |
| `slot` | the ring slot holding the block |

The samples are **not** in the message. Downstream reads them from the ring.

### Credits

The notification stream carries a credit per message in flight; the credit pool's size, the most
notifications downstream can hold at once, is a design parameter. When downstream finishes with a
notification it returns the credit. A credit bounds how much work is queued downstream. It does **not**
protect the block's data: the ring does not wait for readers ([below](#the-ring)).

### Why it always keeps up

- Its writes go to its own memory port, which nothing else uses, at one word a cycle.
- It never reads memory.
- Its notification output never blocks: no credit means a drop, not a wait.
- The state-message input is polled, never waited on.

So it runs at line rate whatever happens downstream, and nothing downstream can make it lose a sample.

## The ring

A **true dual-port BRAM** (`bram_t2p.v`; in pysim `MemSlaveBramWindow`, `waveflow/hw/mm_bram.py`) holding
$R$ slots, each one block of every channel ($B \cdot C \cdot n_{rx}$ samples) plus a header word.

- **Port B: the capture and power detector only**, write only, one word a cycle.
- **Port A: every downstream reader**, over the crossbar.
- **Layout: channel by channel**, `slot[channel][sample][antenna]`. Every job reads one channel's
  block, $B \cdot n_{rx}$ samples, and with this layout that is one contiguous run. If a slot were laid
  out sample by sample with all channels together, a job would read $C$ times the data it needs, on
  the one port every reader shares. Port B can write any address, so the capture places each word in
  its channel's region; this is simplest when **each input word holds one channel's antennas for one
  sample**, which is the requirement on the sample stream.

**The memory has no lock.** The two ports are independent, and a read of the address being written in
the same cycle returns whatever the BRAM's read-during-write mode gives
([BRAM interface](../../guide/interface/primitive/bram.md)). This is what lets the writer never stall,
and it means nothing in the memory stops a reader from reading a slot the writer has started to reuse.
The ring's contract does that instead:

**The ring's contract: the header first, the header last.**

1. **The writer writes the header first.** Each slot's write starts with its header word, the block
   index $n$, then the samples, in order, on its own port.
2. **The reader reads the header last.** A reader takes $n$ and the slot from its notification, reads
   the samples it needs, and **then** reads the slot's header.
3. **If the header still says $n$**, the data was intact for the whole read. **If it says $n + R$**, the
   writer had started overwriting the slot: the job reports **late**, its result is discarded, and the
   lateness is counted.

One check, at the end, is enough. A notification goes out only after block $n$ is completely written.
The slot is next touched when block $n + R$ begins, and its header is the first word written. So if any
sample the reader read had been overwritten, the header was overwritten before it, and the reader's
header read, which comes last, sees it. Two orderings carry this: the writer's writes land in order on
port B, and the reader's header read is issued after its data reads have finished.

**The ring's depth sets how late a read may be.** A slot is overwritten $R$ block periods after it was
written, so a job must finish reading within about $R - 1$ block periods of its block's end. $R$ is a
design parameter: a deeper ring costs BRAM and tolerates more queueing. At the proposed sizes a slot
is $C\, B\, n_{rx} = 32{,}768$ samples, 128 KB at 32 bits a sample, so $R = 8$ is about 1 MB, which fits in the
xczu48dr's URAM.

**Lateness is a measured outcome, not an error.** It is how overload shows up in the ring, alongside
dropped notifications.

### Two checks for the timing model

- **pysim's port B is untimed** (a method call). The capture kernel's own timing model must spread its
  writes over the block period, one word a cycle. Otherwise a whole block appears at one instant in
  pysim, and the overwrite race the header guards against can never occur there.
- **A collision check at RTL.** No error from the memory is visible in the XSI flow today; the check is
  a scan of the trace after the run (`waveflow.utils.bram_trace.find_read_during_write`). A sticky
  `collision` output on the memory, proposed in `plans/rtl_module.md`, would make the header protocol
  checkable in both backends.

## The controller

A hardware kernel between the power detector and the units. **It is not the A53**: at $C$ channels and
a block every $B / f_s$, an interrupt per notification would swamp the processor. The A53 sees a
summary, not the events.

### State

A **table per channel**:

| Field | What |
|---|---|
| state | `absent` or `active` (the detector holds `pending`) |
| $\hat{\ell}$ | the tracked direction, as an index into the direction grid ($\hat{\theta} = \theta_{\hat{\ell}}$) |
| $\hat{\gamma}$ | the SNR estimate |
| last block | the block of the last decision |

and **counters**: detections, stops, late reads, and jobs dispatched per unit type.

### What it does

**On a notification** from the power detector, it turns it into a job:

- **arrival** → an **arrival job** to the systolic pool: `tx_id`, channel, block, slot address;
- **departure** → a **departure job** to the vector pool: the same, plus $\hat{\ell}$ from the table.

The job goes to a pool's `StreamDispatch`, which sends it to a **free unit** (first free). The pool's
credits bound how many jobs wait at each unit. When every unit of a type is busy, the job waits in the
controller.

**On a result**, from either pool's result stream (the controller waits on both):

1. **A late read** → no decision. The detector is told the channel's state is unchanged, so its next
   block notifies again, and the lateness is counted.
2. **Arrival**: the detection test of [a.4](algo.md): $p_{\ell^*} \ge \tau_p$ → `active` with
   $\hat{\ell} = \ell^*$, $\hat{\gamma} = p_{\ell^*} - 1$; otherwise it stays `absent`.
3. **Departure**: the stop test of [b.2](algo.md): $p_b - 1 < \tau_\gamma$ → `absent`; otherwise it
   stays `active`.
4. **Answer the notification**: send the detector a state message for the channel (`active` or
   `absent`, ending its `pending`), and return the notification's credit.

Every notification is answered exactly once, so a channel is never left `pending`.

### The result message

**On every decision**, an arrival detected or a departure declared, the controller emits a **result
message** on its results output:

| Field | What |
|---|---|
| `channel` | $j$ |
| `block` | the block the decision came from |
| `decision` | arrival or departure |
| `theta` | $\hat{\ell}$, the direction grid index (arrival only) |
| `gamma` | $\hat{\gamma}$ (arrival only) |

A search that finds nothing, or a departure check that finds the emitter still there, changes no state
and emits nothing. The **decision time** is measured from the arrival of the block to this message
([what is measured](problem.md#what-is-measured)).

The result messages go to a host-readable queue that the A53 drains without an interrupt per message.
**Every $P$ blocks** the controller also writes a **summary record** (each channel's state,
$\hat{\theta}$ and $\hat{\gamma}$, and the counters since the last record) and raises **one interrupt**,
so the host reads the results and the summary together. $P$ sets how stale the host's view is against
how often it is interrupted; it does not enter the decision time.

### The thresholds it uses

$\tau_p$ (detection) and $\tau_\gamma$ (stop) are memory-mapped registers on the controller, set by the
host like the detector's two. The units compute statistics; only the controller decides.

## The units

Two unit types, each a pool: a `StreamDispatch` in front, a `StreamArbiter` behind, the pattern of
[A pool of workers](../../guide/systems/shared_kernels.md). **Each pool has its own arbiter**, because
the arbiter's `m_sel` (which unit just finished) is that pool's dispatcher's credit; one arbiter across
both pools would give credits that index two dispatchers. The controller reads the two pools' result
streams, waiting on either.

### Systolic units: arrival jobs

One arrival job is the whole search for one block, [a.2 and a.3](algo.md):

1. read the channel's block $X$ ($n_{rx} \times B$) from the ring over the crossbar;
2. form $\hat{Q} = X X^H / B$ on the systolic array;
3. scan the grid: $W = \hat{Q} U$, then $p_\ell = \mathrm{Re}(u_\ell^H w_\ell)$, keeping the largest;
4. read the slot's header;
5. return $\ell^*$, $p_{\ell^*}$ and whether the read was late.

$U$, the $n_{rx} \times L$ steering vectors, is a constant table inside each unit. **$\hat{Q}$ never leaves
the unit**: it is $n_{rx} \times n_{rx}$ (256 values at $n_{rx} = 16$), produced by step 2 and used by step 3
of the same job, so it sits in the unit's own registers or a small BRAM. No shared memory holds it.

### Vector units: departure jobs

One departure job is [b.2](algo.md): read the channel's block, form $u(\theta_{\hat{\ell}})^H X$ (a
$1 \times n_{rx}$ times $n_{rx} \times B$ product) and its squared norm, read the header, and return
$p_b$ and the late flag. It needs $n_{rx}$ complex multiply-accumulates a cycle, so it takes about $B$
cycles of compute, and reads the block once.

**Why not the systolic array for this too.** On an $R \times C$ systolic array, a product with one row
uses one row of the array, about $1/R$ of it, and holds the whole array for the job's length. A
vector unit does the same work at a small fraction of the cost and leaves the systolic units to the
searches.

### What the units share

- **The ring's port A**, over the crossbar. Every job, of both types, reads its block there. That is
  the contended resource: when several channels are searched and tracked at once, the reads queue at
  the port, and a job's time is not its time alone.
- **The controller**, through each pool's arbiter: a result waits there behind the other results of
  its pool.

## The flow of one notification

```text
block n complete ─► detector: channel j past its threshold, credit free ─► notification (j, n, slot)
   ─► controller: arrival → systolic pool / departure → vector pool
   ─► a free unit reads slot n's channel-j block over the crossbar, computes, reads the header
   ─► result ─► controller: the test → state message to the detector, credit returned
   ─► on a decision: a result message (the decision time ends here); the next summary interrupts the A53
```

The **decision time** runs from block $n$'s arrival (its last sample written) to the result message:
waiting for a free unit, the read contended at port A, the computation, the result's return through
its pool's arbiter, and the controller's test. An emitter's **detection delay**, from its onset, adds the
blocks that passed before the decision: blocks before its energy crossed the threshold, and any whose
notification was dropped, whose channel was `pending`, or whose read was late.

## Extensions

- **A shared store for $\hat{Q}$.** Splitting a search into a Gram job and a scan job, so the
  controller can interleave shorter jobs, or averaging $\hat{Q}$ over several blocks for a weak emitter
  (open in [the algorithm](algo.md)), needs $\hat{Q}$ kept between jobs. That would be a local memory
  with its own arbiter shared by the controller and the systolic units, not the ring, whose write port
  is reserved.
- **Mixed pools.** A systolic unit could take departure jobs when idle, at a loss of efficiency.

## Open

- The sample word's layout: one channel's antennas per word is assumed.
- The notification order when credits are short.
- The sizes: the credit pool, the ring's depth $R$, the number of units of each type, the systolic
  array's shape, and the summary period $P$.
- Whether the thresholds become per channel.
